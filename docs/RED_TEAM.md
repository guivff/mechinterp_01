# RED TEAM — “Does on-policy RL leave a readable trace?”

## Bottom line

As written, positive-looking results would not support the intended causal story. The design has no defensible independent-observation count for its headline judge accuracy; the preregistration is visibly unfinished; H2 does not isolate off-policy imitation; H3 does not isolate either generic optimization or reward-specific content; and H4 confounds conditionality with a change of input corpus. The cleanest claims available are descriptive arm contrasts at one seed.

The worst statistical error is treating repeated judge calls as repeated scientific evidence. Each arm and snippet set produces one aggregate vector and therefore one top-20 list. One hundred judgments of that list are ratings of one object, not 100 traces. For steering, the highest independent count is bounded by the number of unique generations and is clustered within 20 prompts; for the training effect, the independent count is one run per arm.

The positive control is also not a literal replication of the cited method. Minder et al. use per-position averages over the first five tokens of 10,000 random-text samples at the middle layer, and steer the fine-tuned model. This design pools positions after skipping the first four tokens over 500 snippets at roughly 0.6 depth and steers the base model. Minder et al. also use Patchscope, which they report as stronger than Logit Lens. A D success would validate this project’s assay, not “replicate Minder”; a D failure would not show the published phenomenon failed. See the [primary paper’s method](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13900v3#S3).

This review is based on `PROJECT_SPEC`, `PREREG`, `NEEL_RUBRIC`, and the cited primary-paper method. Repository code was not included, so every implementation-level check below remains unverified.

## 2. Interpretation traps

### Outcome: A is unreadable

Do not write “GRPO leaves no trace,” “RL is fundamentally different from SFT,” or “reward-specific information is absent.” One mean-vector method, one main layer, one checkpoint, two decoder families, two corpora, and one training seed cannot support those universal negatives. If D failed, the assay was not validated. If A failed Gate 2, there was no successful-RL treatment to study. A nonsignificant or below-threshold readout is not evidence of absence without a detectable-effect bound.

Defensible sentence:

> Conditional on D passing and A improving held-out accuracy, we did not decode A’s norm-matched mean activation difference above lexical and null controls on either preregistered snippet set at the chosen layer; this is evidence against a large D-like readable trace in this run, not evidence that GRPO leaves no trace.

### Outcome: A is readable like C

Do not write “GRPO and SFT create the same representation,” “reward created the trace,” “the traces are equally strong,” or “H2 is disproved.” Similar classification accuracy is not vector equivalence; norm matching deletes magnitude information; and C is generated from A, correctness-filtered, prompt-reweighted, and optimized with a different loss. Failure to observe a 0.15 gap is not an equivalence test.

Defensible sentence:

> After norm matching, A and C had similar neutral-snippet judge accuracy at the preregistered layer, both above the stated lexical and null controls; this fails to show H2’s predicted 0.15 advantage for C in this run, but does not show that GRPO and SFT write the same representation or that reward caused the shared signal.

### Outcome: A is readable only on-domain

Do not write “the trace switches on in math contexts,” “the trace is absent off-domain,” “the mechanism generalizes,” or “this is reward-specific.” A between-corpus contrast is not a causal gate. Math snippets bring digits, formulae, answer syntax, and domain semantics that neutral snippets do not. If TF-IDF or the base/null shows the same gap, the correct interpretation is input-distribution sensitivity or surface-token signal.

Defensible sentence:

> A’s norm-matched mean difference decoded above lexical and null controls on held-out math snippets but not neutral snippets, with the preregistered on-domain advantage at the chosen layer; this is consistent with an input-dependent readout in this run, but does not establish a causal gating mechanism, broad generalization, or reward specificity.

### Outcome: \(A-B\) is readable

Do not write “subtracting B isolates a pure reward vector,” “reward information lives in this direction,” or “the residual explains the accuracy gain.” B is trained under a different stochastic gradient process, not held fixed except for reward assignment; A and B subsequently sample from different policies. Norm matching can inflate a tiny cancellation-sensitive residual, and decodability is not mediation.

Defensible sentence:

> At the preregistered layer, the norm-matched residual \(d_A-d_B\) decoded as math above the stated lexical and null controls; because A and B follow diverging single-run training trajectories, this is a descriptive contrast associated with intact reward assignment, not an isolated reward representation or an explanation of A’s accuracy gain.

## 3. “Strongest evidence against” drafts for form Q14

These are templates, not permission to force a survival narrative. Replace every bracket with the most adverse observed value. If the named control violates a preregistered rule, report that the hypothesis failed instead of using the sentence.

### H1 — lexical baseline

> The strongest evidence against H1 was the TF-IDF control: it scored [L] versus the judge’s [J] on D, leaving a gap of [J−L], only [margin] beyond our 0.10 criterion; D therefore survived the stated lexical gate, but the result remains a modified, single-run positive-control assay rather than a literal Minder replication.

### H2 — neighboring-layer robustness

> The strongest evidence against H2 was the preregistered [L−4 or L+4] check, where C’s neutral-text advantage over A fell from [primary-layer gap] to [most adverse gap]; the predicted direction survived there, but its size was layer-dependent and the A–C contrast still does not isolate imitation from data selection or treatment dose.

### H3 — untrained-LoRA null

> The strongest evidence against H3 was N3: its matched-parameter perturbation produced [null norm/cosine/readability] closest to B’s [corresponding value]; B nevertheless exceeded that control by [difference] and met the preregistered geometry criteria, although this does not turn B into a generic-optimization control or A−B into a causal reward component.

### H4 — lexical on-domain advantage

> The strongest evidence against H4 was the TF-IDF control, which reproduced an on-domain-minus-neutral advantage of [L] compared with the judge’s [J], leaving only [J−L] beyond linear lexical cues; H4 survived the preregistered surface-signal rule by [margin], so we describe an input-dependent readout rather than a learned context gate.

## 4. Scope cuts at hour 8 of 17

### Cut arm C

Treat stretch arm C′ as already out of scope; it should never have been started under time pressure. The first substantive arm to cut is C. It supports only H2, cannot start until A’s final policy exists, then requires eight-way sampling, rejection/filtering, a new SFT run, and the complete readout stack. Worse, even a perfect C run does not isolate the claimed mechanism because it changes the teacher policy, correctness selection, prompt difficulty weights, token masking, loss, and functional update dose. Cutting C drops H2 but preserves the central A question, D’s assay gate, and B’s imperfect but directly reward-assignment-related contrast.

### Cut steering

J-Lens should already have been dropped under its 20-minute rule; it is not the substantive triage decision. The first full readout to cut is steering. It is generation- and judge-heavy, the coefficient and injection schedule are under-specified, and it introduces fluency failure and winner’s-curse artifacts. Preserve geometry, the fixed logit-lens token readout, null distributions, behavioral validation, and the cheap self-report baseline. Record the cut as a preregistration deviation: H1’s current “both modalities” criterion can no longer pass, so D may be called a token-readout positive control, not a full H1 replication.

## 5. Three questions Neel would ask in the first minute

1. **“What are the independent observations behind Figure 1?”** Is there one seed, one aggregate vector, and one top-20 list per cell, or 100 genuinely different traces? If the former, why is judge-call repetition presented as accuracy with an experimental sample size?

2. **“What did D actually validate?”** Why is it called a Minder replication when the token positions, number of random samples, layer, decoder mix, and steered target differ—and did D, the lexical baseline, the nulls, and A’s paired behavioral gate all pass before any A interpretation?

3. **“Exactly what causal quantity do B and C identify?”** Why is shuffled-reward random-gradient drift “generic optimization,” why is the difference between two diverging on-policy runs “reward-specific,” and how can correctness-filtered distillation from final A isolate imitation from data selection, prompt difficulty, and update magnitude?

## Claim ceiling if nothing else changes

- **H1:** At most, D passes or fails this project’s modified single-run assay. Do not call it a literal replication.
- **H2:** At most, A and C differ under two bundled training procedures. Do not attribute the gap to on-policy RL versus off-policy imitation.
- **H3:** At most, A and B have specified geometric relations in these runs. Do not call B generic optimization or \(A-B\) reward-specific.
- **H4:** At most, A’s readout differs between two input corpora. Do not call the contrast a causal context gate.

If the executive summary makes stronger claims than these without repairing the corresponding rows above, a skeptical reviewer can dismiss the positive result without disputing a single plotted number.
